# Competitor Copy Audit — MTM vs. Numeris, Comscore, Nielsen

Scope: MTM's current homepage + insights page copy (`src/content/copy/home.json`, `insights.json`) against the public marketing copy on numeris.ca, comscore.com, and nielsen.com. Goal: SEO/positioning input — not a page build.

---

## TL;DR

MTM's copy is accurate and calm but reads as a **category description** ("The Standard for..."), while all three competitors open with an **outcome or rhythm-driven claim** (Nielsen's "Real audiences. Real data. Real decisions.", Comscore's "Measure what matters..."). MTM also owns a genuinely unique wedge — the only Canada-specific, bilingual, policy/funding-accepted media+tech+demographic dataset — but that claim is buried in an FAQ answer instead of leading the hero. Scale proof (300K+ surveyed, 41M Canadians mapped) is real and strong but split across two different sections instead of front-loaded like Nielsen's panel-size stat.

## Gaps and observations

### 1. Headline is descriptive, not propulsive
"The Standard for Canadian Media & Technology Insights" states a category position (we are *the standard*) rather than a benefit or outcome. All three competitors use an active verb or rhythmic triplet in the hero (Nielsen's repeated "Real ___.", Comscore's "Measure what matters"). MTM's own FAQ-buried language — "evidence, not assumptions" — is a sharper contrast device than the current hero headline and could be promoted.

### 2. Scale proof is split, not stacked
- Nielsen puts its biggest number (750K+ panelists, 57 countries) right at the trust-signal layer near the hero.
- MTM has two strong numbers — "300,000+ Canadians 18+ Surveyed" (hero) and "41 Million Canadians Mapped" (buried in the Data Products package section further down) — that never appear together. Stacking them near the hero would read closer to Nielsen's scale claim.

### 3. The real differentiator is in an FAQ, not the pitch
FAQ #3 states: *"MTM data is widely used across the Canadian media, advertising, academic, and public sectors to support publications, strategic planning, funding applications, and policy development."* This is MTM's actual point of difference from Comscore/Nielsen (global generalists) and Numeris (broadcast-audience only, not full media+tech+demographic): **Canada-specific, bilingual, government/funding-accepted data**. None of the three competitors can make this exact claim. Right now it only surfaces to someone who scrolls to FAQ #3 — it should be load-bearing in the hero or the "Access Trusted Research On" section.

### 4. CTA language doesn't vary by intent
MTM uses "Request a Demo" identically in both the hero and the closing CTA. Competitors vary CTA verbs by funnel stage/intent: Nielsen ("Get a demo" / "Contact us"), Numeris ("Become a Client" / "Have a Question?"), Comscore ("Learn more" / "Take me there"). Repeating the identical CTA twice on one page is a missed chance to speak to a colder vs. warmer visitor.

### 5. No named flagship product/umbrella term
Numeris has "OneLook," Nielsen has "Nielsen ONE" — a single proper-noun platform name that becomes memorable and citable. MTM has "The MTM Suite," which is close but functions as a section label rather than a marketed platform name. Not necessarily a gap to fix in copy alone (it's a product-naming question), but worth flagging since competitors are visibly using this device.

### 6. Structural device competitors use that MTM doesn't
Comscore organizes its whole value prop around three verbs — Plan / Transact / Evaluate — mapping directly to how a buyer moves through the sales cycle. MTM's three "Access Trusted Research On" items (Technology Trends, Media Behaviour, 5,000+ Variables) are topic buckets, not a buyer-journey structure. Not wrong, just a different (weaker) organizing principle — worth considering if the section is rewritten.

---

## What MTM is already doing well (don't change)

- "Evidence, not assumptions" is a genuinely good contrast line — better than anything found on the three competitor sites, which lean on adjectives ("trusted," "comprehensive") rather than a real juxtaposition.
- Product naming (MTM 18+, MTM Junior, MTM Newcomers, MTM Census Tool) is clear and audience-segmented — comparable to how Nielsen names sub-products, better than Comscore's more abstract categories.
- "Request a Demo" as a primary CTA is standard and correct — no change needed there, only the repetition issue in #4 above.

---

## Suggested next step

If useful, the highest-leverage rewrite is the hero: replace the category-claim headline with a version that (a) keeps "evidence, not assumptions" or a similar contrast device, and (b) pulls the FAQ #3 policy/funding-acceptance claim up into hero or immediate-trust-signal territory, since it's the one claim none of Numeris, Comscore, or Nielsen can make.
